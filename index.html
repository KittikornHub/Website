About

Research with an operational and socio-technical perspective

My work examines how digital technologies can strengthen visibility, coordination, trust, and decision-making in healthcare supply chains. I am particularly interested in the conditions under which traceability technologies create practical value for healthcare professionals and organisations.

My academic interests sit at the intersection of supply chain management, information systems, healthcare logistics, technology adoption, governance, and operations research.

Current doctoral research

Blockchain adoption for vaccine traceability in Thailand

My current study investigates factors influencing healthcare professionals’ intention to use blockchain to enhance traceability in vaccine cold chains, with a focus on hospital pharmaceutical departments in Thailand.

01

Research problem

Vaccine traceability depends on trustworthy information, operational coordination, professional acceptance, and organisational readiness, not technology alone.

02

Analytical foundation

The study draws on the Technology Acceptance Model and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology.

03

Key constructs

Trust and transparency, traceability and efficiency, organisational support, user acceptance, and behavioural intention.

04

Expected contribution

A context-sensitive understanding of how technical capability, governance, and user readiness align in Thai healthcare settings.
